# Introduction {#sec:interface}

Every ambient line-bundle index on CIPro record {{INDEX_DIVISIBILITY_WITNESS_FIRST_ID}} is even. On record {{INDEX_DIVISIBILITY_WITNESS_SECOND_ID}}, the restricted hyperplane bundle $\mathcal{O}_X(0,0,1)$ has index $5$. The records share Hodge numbers $(3,47)$, Euler characteristic $-88$, and the same four divisibilities of the cubic intersection and second Chern forms. For a line bundle pulled back through a free quotient, its index upstairs equals the degree of the cover times its index downstairs. The index-$5$ bundle therefore fails the necessary divisibility test for any even-degree free quotient of that threefold. Evenness on the first record leaves the existence of a free action and the equivariance of a proposed bundle to be checked separately. [@cipro-release; @anderson-heterotic-scan]

## What a configuration determines

A complete-intersection Calabi–Yau (CICY) threefold is presented here by a matrix of equation degrees in a product of projective spaces. Candelas and collaborators enumerated such presentations, and Green, Hübsch, and Lütken computed their Hodge numbers. He and Candelas then asked how many of the matrices could be separated by integral topology and used cubic cup products and Pontryagin pairings to distinguish manifolds that share Hodge numbers. We follow that line of inquiry on the pinned CIPro archive. The degree matrix determines the intersection tensor and second Chern pairings on the restricted hyperplane classes. Riemann–Roch combines the cubic and linear terms into the line-bundle index. Finite differences give its fixed divisor, the gcd of its values on the entire lattice generated by those restrictions. The differing gcds rule out an integral isomorphism of the two ambient lattices that preserves both forms [@candelas-cicy; @green-hodge; @he-candelas-count; @he-yao-yau-topology; @hirzebruch-riemann-roch]. The full audit appears in [@sec:integral_invariants].

For a comparison of the underlying manifolds, the ambient classes must be related to the full integral lattice. The smooth complete intersections of degrees $(3,3)$ in $\mathbf{P}^5$ and $(3,2,2)$ in $\mathbf{P}^6$ share Hodge pair ${{INTEGRAL_WITNESS_HODGE}}$. In these two cases integral weak Lefschetz makes the hyperplane class a primitive generator. Its cube evaluates to ${{INTEGRAL_WITNESS_FIRST_CUBIC}}$ and ${{INTEGRAL_WITNESS_SECOND_CUBIC}}$, respectively. A homeomorphism would preserve the absolute value of that evaluation, so these two threefolds cannot be homeomorphic. When ambient divisors miss classes or generate a subgroup of finite index, the same computation gives a comparison of the supplied sublattices [@lefschetz-analysis-situs; @wall-six-manifolds; @cipro-release]. The rank-one contractions are given in [@thm:integral_content_witness].

## Why equations and counting units matter

Giving every presentation equal weight yields a median Euler characteristic of ${{ATLAS_PRESENTATION_MEDIAN}}$ in the non-product archive. Giving every recorded Hodge pair equal total weight shifts the median to ${{ATLAS_PAIR_MEDIAN}}$. The first measure describes the result of drawing a presentation uniformly, and the second describes drawing a Hodge pair uniformly. For an embedding-sensitive quantity, such as the degree of a selected ample class, the choice of presentation also fixes which class is selected. A published ineffective split produces a different all-ones Hilbert polynomial for the same deformation type [@anderson-fibrations]. The census and the split calculation appear in [@sec:catalog] and [@sec:scholarship], respectively.

A degree matrix can also hide a jump when the equations vary. For $\mathcal{O}_X(3,-3)$ on the bicubic, the line-bundle index is zero throughout the smooth family. The middle Koszul map has rank ten on the generic smooth member and rank three on the specific smooth equation used here. Its kernel and cokernel give $h^1=h^2={{BICUBIC_EXACT_WITNESS_PAIRED_EXCESS}}$ on that member, whereas both dimensions vanish generically. Individual dimensions are required in heterotic line-bundle searches that use the index to screen net chirality and then test whether particular multiplets are present. The native rank experiment gives exact dimensions for one specified bicubic and charge grid. In higher codimension the Koszul computation may also require higher differentials [@constantin-cohomology; @wang-hilbert-cohomology; @anderson-heterotic-scan]. [@thm:bicubic_jump] gives the exact example.

## From configuration data to a backend result

A geometry is a mapping containing ambient dimensions and configuration degrees. Defining equations, line-bundle sums, and symmetry generators can be added as needed. In the original API, `CIProp` requests properties and their prerequisites, while `CIMod` constructs modified objects or operation-specific results. In Python these correspond to `property` and `modify`. The `call` and `call_result` methods address public functions by name. Full property results can include both the input and additional fields [@anderson-cipro; @cipro-release]. To recompute an invariant independently of the archive, construct a fresh association from `DimPs` and `Conf`. A populated association can reuse its precomputed keys.

The Python path uses native integers and SymPy for exact algebra [@sympy]. Keyword options retain the spelling of the exported interface. For the Wolfram path, mappings become associations, lists become lists, and keyword names become string-keyed options. Wolfram Expression Format (WXF) preserves symbolic heads and exact rationals across transport. The diagnostic result includes messages and printed output, which can be material when reproducing a failed or partially evaluated symbolic computation [@wolfram-client].

The released catalog contains {{RELEASED_TOTAL}} entries across public functions, keys for properties, and keys for modifications. Python registers {{NATIVE_TOTAL}} of these in supported domains. Several keys call shared operations. [@tbl:method_inventory] counts the registered entries, and the language guides define their mathematical domains.

## Replaying an archive record and a method preset

Start `py-cipro-dashboard` and open the loopback address printed by the launcher. Select archive record {{QUINTIC_RECORD_ID}} and compute `CIDim`, `CY`, and `Euler` from the configuration association. For this quintic the results are $3$, true, and ${{QUINTIC_EULER}}$. The record inspector identifies which values were computed and which were read from the archive. In the method lab, load and edit a preset, run the method, and inspect scalar, numeric matrix, or symbolic results. Symbolic arguments use a constrained JSON representation for symbols, indices, rationals, and arithmetic. The result identifies the actual backend, including when Auto resolves in Python. Procedural details and individual supported domains are in the repository's dashboard guide and Python/Wolfram concordance.

The computation below also records the backend without a browser. The association contains only the defining data.

~~~python
from py_cipro import CIPro

quintic = {"DimPs": [4], "Conf": [[5]]}
with CIPro(backend="python") as ci:
    result = ci.call_result(
        "CIProp", quintic, ["CIDim", "CY", "Euler"]
    )
    assert result.backend == "python"
    assert (result.value["CIDim"], result.value["CY"],
            result.value["Euler"]) == (3, True, -200)
~~~
