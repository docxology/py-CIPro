# Scope in the literature {#sec:scholarship}

## Catalogue lineage and the unit of enumeration

Candelas, Dale, Lütken, and Schimmrigk enumerated complete-intersection presentations in products of projective spaces. Green, Hübsch, and Lütken supplied Hodge data. The Oxford distribution makes that lineage accessible in text and Mathematica formats, and Gagnon and Ho-Kim published a separate enumeration. The statistics in [@sec:catalog] count the IDs and use the field encodings of the released CIPro text member. Repeating the census on a different catalogue requires matching its records and checking how it encodes products. [@candelas-cicy; @green-hodge; @oxford-cicy3-data; @gagnon-hokim; @cipro-release]

A single smooth threefold can admit several complete-intersection descriptions. Anderson, Gao, Gray, and Lee construct new favorable descriptions in 2,946 cases by changing the embedding. Their procedure uses ineffective splittings. A column is replaced by several columns and an extra projective factor, and the two descriptions have a common smooth determinantal locus in complex-structure moduli. Identification of the deformation types relies on this smooth common locus and the specified splitting. They use the new embeddings to find descended divisor bases. Their Kähler-favorability criterion also asks whether the whole Kähler cone descends, a further requirement in the use of ambient divisors to classify fibrations and flops. [@anderson-fibrations; @brodie-flops]

## Integral topology and the choice of polarization

::: {.proposition #prop:integral_topological_comparison title="Integral data for oriented six-manifold comparison"}
For an oriented closed smooth six-manifold $X$, let $L_X=H^2(X;\mathbb Z)$ and use the oriented fundamental class $[X]$ to define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_X(u,v,w)&=\langle u\smile v\smile w,[X]\rangle,\\
\rho_X(u)&=\langle p_1(TX)\smile u,[X]\rangle .
\end{aligned}
$$ {#eq:integral_topological_data}

For simply connected spin six-manifolds with torsion-free homology, Wall's classification identifies the oriented diffeomorphism type from $b_3$ and the pair $(\mu,\rho)$ on $L$. Thus, besides equality of $b_3$, it requires one integral isomorphism $\phi:L_X\to L_Y$ satisfying $\mu_X(u,v,w)=\mu_Y(\phi u,\phi v,\phi w)$ and $\rho_X(u)=\rho_Y(\phi u)$ for all $u,v,w$. Jupp extends the classification beyond the spin case for closed, oriented, simply connected six-manifolds with torsion-free homology. [@wall-six-manifolds; @jupp-six-manifolds]
:::

He and Candelas used integral intersection and Pontryagin data to distinguish configurations sharing Hodge numbers. Examining embeddings in fewer than six projective spaces gave a lower bound of {{LITERATURE_CICY_TOPOLOGY_LOWER_BOUND}} distinct manifolds. He, Yao, and Yau use four divisibility invariants of two forms. The three cubic invariants take progressively more restricted evaluations, and $d_p$ uses evaluations of the second Chern class. Their explicit tensor algorithm assumes favorable geometry, and their prediction experiment compares learned estimates against computed labels. The exact census in [@sec:integral_invariants] uses the ambient image furnished by the archive, including presentations where that image misses divisor directions. The full-lattice analogues require both full rank and primitivity of the image. [@he-candelas-count; @he-yao-yau-topology]

Fixed-divisor theory gives a separate arithmetic comparison. Bhargava expresses the ideal generated by the values of a univariate polynomial on a subset of a Dedekind domain using generalized factorials. Rajkumar, Reddy, and Semwal extend finite-value formulas and coefficient descriptions to several variables. Here the lattice charges range over $\mathbb Z^r$ and Riemann–Roch produces a rational cubic with integer values. The product binomial basis in [@prop:ambient_index_fixed_divisor] turns its value ideal into a coefficient gcd. The symmetric cubic and the Chern term leave a short list of coefficients to check. The resulting gcd records a joint congruence of the two forms, which can persist even when their separate contents agree across two records. [@bhargava-fixed-divisors; @rajkumar-multivariate-fixed-divisors; @watt-fixed-divisor]

On a Calabi–Yau threefold, $p_1(TX)=c_1(TX)^2-2c_2(TX)=-2c_2(TX)$. The quantities in [@eq:polarized_pairings] then give $\mu_X(\ell,\ell,\ell)$ and $-\rho_X(\ell)/2$ at the chosen ambient class $\ell$. This identifies the part of the integral forms that the polarized audit measures. A different embedding can choose a different class on the same manifold, and ambient combinations may span only part of the integral lattice. Comparing full lattices calls for integral generators, including the classes beyond the ambient image where applicable. [@wall-six-manifolds; @he-candelas-count; @anderson-fibrations]

Chandra and collaborators quantify the gap between arithmetic tests and decisions of integral equivalence for hypersurfaces from the Kreuzer–Skarke list. At Picard number three their polynomial invariants and Hodge numbers separate 150 classes. Adding greatest-common-divisor invariants raises that lower bound to 171, while solving the integral basis equations gives 183 classes in the stated sample. They also constrain candidate basis changes by preserving the cubic and linear contributions to the line-bundle index. These are the same two contributions in [@eq:line_index], now evaluated on many lattice vectors and tested under one basis change. The ambient cell census measures separation among presentations. The full-lattice witness in [@thm:integral_content_witness] supplies a ring obstruction because the rank-one generators are primitive. [@chandra-enumerating-topology]

## Rank strata, fixed charges, and generic cohomology

The index in [@eq:line_index] depends on characteristic classes. The dimensions in the long exact sequence can also depend on the defining coefficients through the rank of a multiplication map. A nonzero minor evaluated at a specified equation supplies a rank lower bound there and over the generic coefficient field. An exact rank certificate needs a matching upper bound, supplied by matrix dimensions, vanishing larger minors, or exact row reduction. At charge $(3,-3)$ the middle map is a square coefficient matrix of size ten, so its determinant cuts out the rank-drop locus within the smooth parameter space. On that locus the two middle dimensions both equal the rank deficiency by [@prop:bicubic_koszul_ranks]. [@constantin-cohomology; @anderson-cohomology-jumping; @wang-hilbert-cohomology]

::: {.proposition #prop:finite_grid_maximal_rank title="Simultaneous generic ranks on a finite charge grid"}
Let $U$ be the nonempty smooth locus in the projective parameter space of complex bicubic equations. For each charge $q$ in a finite grid $G$, let $M_q(F)$ be the relevant multiplication map of [@prop:bicubic_koszul_ranks] and $r_q$ its maximum rank on $U$. All these ranks are attained together on the nonempty Zariski-open subset

$$
U_G=\bigcap_{q\in G}\{[F]\in U:\operatorname{rank} M_q(F)=r_q\}.
$$ {#eq:finite_grid_generic_rank}

The entries of each map are linear in the defining coefficients. The complement of the maximum-rank locus is cut out by the $r_q$-minors for $r_q>0$. A zero maximum rank imposes no condition. Each maximum-rank locus is dense in the irreducible space $U$, so their finite intersection is nonempty. [@wang-hilbert-cohomology]
:::

Constantin and Lukas infer generic chamber polynomials from explicit Koszul calculations and report checking the bicubic formulas across a bounded charge box. Their Section 2.2 places the anti-diagonal on the boundary of the chambers. Wang proves the generic bicubic rank statement by evaluating a critical determinant at the singular section $(\sum_i x_i y_i)^3$. After a monomial rescaling the associated operator is the cube of an $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ lowering operator, which is invertible between the weight $3$ and weight $-3$ spaces. Nonvanishing transfers to a nonempty open set meeting the smooth locus. Thus the singular section certifies a generic rank, and the smooth pure-cube section in [@thm:bicubic_smoothness] certifies a rank drop at a particular member. Passing from a finite charge grid to the whole charge lattice makes the intersection in [@eq:finite_grid_generic_rank] countable, giving a very general condition over $\mathbb C$. [@constantin-cohomology; @wang-hilbert-cohomology]

Higher codimension calls for different certificates. Larfors and Schneider classify polynomial regions from explicit cohomology calculations on selected Picard-rank-two CICYs. In Wang's codimension-two example of degrees $(1,2)$ and $(1,3)$ in $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^4$, the middle Koszul column for charge $(0,b)$ is acyclic for $b\ge5$. The next differential is multiplication by the determinant of the two equations' $\mathbb P^1$ coefficient matrix. For a nonzero determinant this map subtracts the dimension of the degree-$(b-5)$ polynomial space from the section count. Extending the single-map bicubic replay in [@sec:bundles] to higher codimension therefore requires tracking the higher differentials. [@larfors-schneider-cohomology; @wang-hilbert-cohomology]

## Index filters and spectrum tests in line-bundle searches

In their scan of heterotic $SU(5)$ models, Anderson and collaborators first use $\operatorname{ind}(V)=-3|\Gamma|$ to select an upstairs line-bundle sum $V$ with the net chirality needed for three families after a free quotient by $\Gamma$. Their subsequent tests impose $h^2(X,V)=0$ for the absence of anti-families and $h^2(X,\bigwedge^2V)>0$ for at least one Higgs pair. The latter two tests involve individual cohomology dimensions, which can vary with the equations. Their Equations (4.7)–(4.9) and Table 1 give a concrete case in which the separate cohomology calculations refine the candidates selected by the index. Section 4.4 applies divisibility to each line-bundle product when the summands are individually equivariant, and to the corresponding blocks when the equivariant structure mixes summands. [@anderson-heterotic-scan]

The CIPro manual illustrates this dependence in Section 3.2 using a codimension-two CICY. The cohomology dimensions of the sum $\mathcal{O}_X(1,-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_X(-5,2)$ are $(0,3,4,0)$ with generated equations and $(0,4,5,0)$ with the specified smooth equations. Both give index $1$. The authors trace the jump to the second summand. The bicubic in [@thm:bicubic_jump] realizes the same mechanism in a hypersurface, with a larger jump for a single line bundle. Computing a spectrum after a free quotient would additionally require the group action on cohomology. The model calculations of Anderson and collaborators use those representations to extract the multiplets that survive the quotient and Wilson line. [@anderson-cipro; @anderson-heterotic-line-bundles]
